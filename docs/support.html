Thunderbolt Budget

Support

Thunderbolt Budget shows which devices are plugged into each Thunderbolt port on your Mac, and how much of each port's bandwidth they're using right now.

Requirements

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later). Intel Macs are not supported.
  • macOS 26 or later.

Common questions

Why does a port show 0 Gbps when a fast drive is plugged in?

The port's pill and bar show live usage: data actually moving right now. An idle drive uses no bandwidth, even if it connected at 40 Gb/s. Start a copy and the number will go up.

What's the difference between a device's speed and the port's usage?

Each device shows the link speed it connected at in a blue pill, for example "40 Gb/s". That's the most it could use. Devices on the same port share that port's connection to your Mac, so link speeds don't add up. The port's coloured pill shows what's actually in use, out of the port's capacity.

Why does a port with a display show usage even when nothing is copying?

Displays reserve bandwidth all the time to send the picture. Thunderbolt Budget estimates this from the display's native resolution and refresh rate and includes it in the port's usage. A 4K display at 60 Hz uses roughly 14 Gbps.

My display's resolution looks different from what I set in System Settings.

If your display is scaled (for example a 4K display set to "looks like 2560 × 1440"), the app shows the "Looks Like" size you chose and the panel's Native Pixels separately. Bandwidth is based on the native pixels, because that's what the cable carries.

Which traffic is counted?

Reads and writes on external drives, and traffic through external network adapters (such as a Thunderbolt 10GbE adapter), plus the estimated display bandwidth. Your Mac's internal drive, built-in Ethernet and Wi-Fi are not counted, because they don't use the Thunderbolt ports. Devices such as audio interfaces don't report a byte count to macOS, so they appear in the list without a live figure.

How do bottleneck alerts work?

You'll get a notification when a single port's live usage crosses the threshold in Settings (36 Gbps by default for a 40 Gbps port; it scales up automatically for 80 Gbps Thunderbolt 5 ports). You'll get one alert per busy period, not a stream of them. You can change the threshold or turn alerts off in Settings.

I'm not getting alerts.

Check that Bottleneck alerts is on in the app's Settings, and that notifications for Thunderbolt Budget are allowed in System Settings ▸ Notifications. If they've been turned off there, the app's Settings window shows a note with a button to open the right page.

Why is my port called "Thunderbolt Port 1" instead of "Left Back Port"?

Physical port names are only known for some Mac models. On others, ports are numbered instead. The devices and figures are the same either way.

How do I get the main window back after closing it?

Click the bolt icon in the menu bar and choose Open Thunderbolt Budget. You can also open Settings from there.

Does the app collect any data?

No. Everything stays on your Mac, and the app has no network access. See the Privacy Policy.
